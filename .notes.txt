I am going to be updating the indexes as there is a flaw in the current method. Currently, we use a single index.
The vectors in this index are described by one-second updates in the order book stream.
Each one-second order book aggregate contains three items:
1. Mid price delta (signed for going up or down)
2. Spread delta (between bid/ask)
3. Volume

My indexes currently use the first two like this: { mid_delta, spread_delta, mid_delta, spread_delta, [...] }.

This is flawed because while making future predictions, the futures have a tendancy to disagree. The weighted averages of those futures
often average out. This causes the future deltas to consistently under-report the magnitude of the coming changes. And since the
direction of the movement is coupled with the magnitude, it too cannot be reliably predicted. (The best we got was around 51-56% accuracy).

The future implementation is going to separate these two points into their own indexes and we are also going to predict them separately.
The first is the magnitude index. We are going to drop the spread_delta and focus only on the mid_delta (midpoint between best bid/ask).
Additionally, these deltas are going to be their absolute values. In this index, we are not going to be tracking direction, but just
absolute price movement. When we perform future predictions later, this should theoretically solve the problem of under reporting magnitudes.

The second index is going to be only for the direction with three values: -1.0, 0, 1.0 for down, neutral, or up. 
It'll be interesting to see how accurate this one can become since there are only three possible values in a given sequence of size k vector.

Using these two indexes together should hopefully produce more accurate futures, and dropping the spread_delta could reduce noise and focus on the metrics
we actually care about right now.
